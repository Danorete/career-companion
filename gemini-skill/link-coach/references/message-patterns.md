# Message patterns

Fill the brackets only with facts the student gave you. Keep it short. Ask the student to edit
the wording so it sounds like them. Link never sends anything.

## 1. Follow-up after a conversation (four parts)

```
Hi [Name], thanks again for [the chat / your time / your talk] on [day].
[One specific detail: "Your point about ___ stuck with me."]
[Something useful: "Here is the link I mentioned" / "I tried ___ and it helped."]
No need to reply. I just wanted to say thanks!
```

Notes: Callback, One detail, Useful to them, No pressure.

## 2. First message to someone you have not met

Use only when there is a real reason.

```
Hi [Name], I'm a [year] studying [subject] at [school]. I [read / saw / heard] your
[talk / post / project] on [topic], and [one specific thing] helped me think about [one line].
Would you have 15 minutes in the next few weeks for a quick chat about how you got into [field]?
If you're busy, no worries at all.
```

Rules: under 80 words, one ask, easy to decline, no attachments, no résumé in the first message.

## 3. Thank-you after help or an introduction

```
Hi [Name], I wanted to let you know [what happened because of their help].
Thank you for [the specific thing they did]. I'll keep you posted on [next step].
```

## 4. Asking for help (advice, review, or an introduction)

```
Hi [Name], I'm working on [specific thing]. You mentioned [detail from before], so I thought
of you. Would you be open to [one specific small ask: a 15-minute chat / a look at my project
summary / a pointer on where to look]? I'll make it easy: [offer a time window or a short doc].
```

## 5. Reconnecting after silence

```
Hi [Name], it's been a few weeks. Quick update: [one specific thing you did since last time].
I thought of you because [link to something they said]. No need to reply.
```

Stop after two tries.

## Keeping a promise ("I'll send you the article")

```
Hi [Name], here is the [article / link / dataset] I mentioned: [link].
[One sentence on why you chose it.] Hope it helps!
```

## Avoid

- "I hope this email finds you well", "pick your brain", "I'd love to connect" with nothing else
- Asking for a job or internship in the first message
- Long paragraphs, multiple asks, or flattery without a detail
- Made-up shared experiences or fake enthusiasm

## Recap card (the student copies this into their own notes)

```
Name:
Where we met / when:
What we talked about:
What I promised:
Next step and date:
```
